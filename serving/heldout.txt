The fisherman came up the path in the morning with a basket of fish. He knocked on the door and the keeper opened it.

"Good morning," said the fisherman. "The sea was rough in the night, but your light was bright and we saw the rocks."

"Thank you," said the keeper. "Do come in and sit by the window. The wind is cold today."

They sat by the window and looked at the grey water. The gulls were loud over the boats, and the waves broke white on the stones below.

In the spring the girl planted flowers by the door. She carried water up the path from the well, and every evening she climbed the stairs with the keeper to light the great lamp. He showed her how to clean the lens and how to turn the wheel slowly.

One summer night the sky was clear and the sea was calm. The girl sat at the top of the tower and counted the boats. One boat, two boats, five boats, ten boats. All of them came home to the village before the dark.

"Why do you write the weather every day?" asked the girl.

"So that one day somebody will read it," said the keeper, "and know that the light was always there."

In the autumn a storm broke the old window at the top of the stairs. The keeper and the girl worked all morning to mend it. They used wood from the boat house and nails from the little tin under the bed. When the work was done, the girl wrote in the book: the window is new, and the light is safe.
