The old lighthouse keeper woke before the sun every morning. He climbed the hundred stairs, cleaned the great glass lens, and wrote the weather in his little book. The wind was cold and the sea was grey. The gulls were loud and the boats were slow.

One morning a small girl came up the path from the village. She carried a basket of bread and a jar of honey. She knocked on the door and the keeper opened it.

"Good morning," said the girl. "My mother sent you bread and honey."

"Good morning," said the keeper. "Thank you, and thank your mother. Will you come in and see the light?"

The girl smiled and followed him up the stairs. One step, two steps, ten steps, fifty steps, one hundred steps. At the top the room was bright and round, and the great lens shone like a crystal in the sun.

"How does it work?" asked the girl.

"At night I light the lamp," said the keeper. "The lens takes the small light and makes it strong. The light turns and turns, and the ships at sea can see it. When they see the light, they know where the rocks are, and they stay away."

"Do you ever sleep?" asked the girl.

"I sleep in the day, a little," said the keeper. "But at night I watch. The light must never go out."

The girl looked out of the window. She saw the sea, the rocks, the village, and the long white road. She saw a fishing boat far away, and a cloud shaped like a whale.

"I want to be a keeper when I grow up," she said.

The keeper laughed. "Then you must learn to climb the stairs, to clean the lens, to read the wind, and to write the weather in the book. And you must learn to stay awake when the night is long."

Every morning after that, the girl came up the path. She brought bread, or honey, or apples, or fish. She climbed the stairs with the keeper. She cleaned the lens with a soft cloth. She learned the names of the winds and the shapes of the clouds. She wrote the weather in the little book in her own careful hand.

The wind was cold. The wind was warm. The sea was grey. The sea was blue. The gulls were loud. The boats were slow. The light turned and turned.

One winter night a great storm came from the north. The wind screamed and the rain beat on the windows. The waves were tall as houses and they broke white on the rocks. The keeper was old and his legs were tired, and he could not climb the stairs fast enough.

The girl saw the storm from her window in the village. She put on her coat and her boots and she ran up the path in the dark. She opened the door and climbed the stairs. One step, two steps, ten steps, fifty steps, one hundred steps. At the top the lamp was low and the light was weak.

She cleaned the lens. She filled the lamp. She turned the wheel. The light grew strong and bright, and it turned and turned over the dark and angry sea.

Far away, a fishing boat was lost in the storm. The men on the boat were cold and afraid. Then they saw the light. They knew where the rocks were, and they turned away from them, and they came home safe to the village.

In the morning the storm was gone. The sea was calm and the sky was clean. The keeper climbed the stairs slowly, one step at a time, and found the girl asleep by the lamp. The light was still burning.

He put a blanket over her and sat down by the window. He opened the little book and wrote the weather for the night: wind from the north, very strong. Rain. Big waves. Then, under it, he wrote one more line: the light did not go out.

Many years later, the keeper was gone, and the girl was a woman. She lived in the lighthouse now. Every morning she woke before the sun. She climbed the hundred stairs, cleaned the great glass lens, and wrote the weather in the little book.

And one morning a small boy came up the path from the village, carrying a basket of bread and a jar of honey.

"Good morning," said the boy.

"Good morning," said the keeper. "Will you come in and see the light?"
